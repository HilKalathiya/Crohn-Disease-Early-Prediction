AI-based early prediction of Crohn's disease severity

See Crohn's damage early,
grade it consistently.

CrohnSight reads small-bowel capsule-endoscopy frames, finds Crohn's lesions — from the earliest aphthoid ulcers to large ulcers and strictures — and turns them into a severity grade a clinician can check. Two add-on modules extend the idea to ulcerative colitis and to forecasting flares from routine follow-up data.

Main module · CrohnIPI
Crohn's Lesion Analyzer →

Upload one capsule-endoscopy frame. A 10-model EfficientNet-B2 ensemble names the lesion (7 classes), grades severity on a 4-step scale, and shows a heat-map of where it looked.

Clinical workflow
Capsule Study Triage →

Upload a whole set of frames (or a .zip). Every frame is scored and the study is ranked so the reader sees the most severe frames first — capsule videos hold tens of thousands of frames.

Why this matters

Crohn's disease is a lifelong inflammatory bowel disease that often starts in the small bowel, where a normal colonoscopy cannot reach. Capsule endoscopy films the whole small bowel, but a single study produces tens of thousands of frames that a specialist must read by eye — slow, tiring, and graded differently by different readers (in the CrohnIPI study, three experts agreed only moderately on the exact lesion type, κ = 0.57).

Catching small lesions such as aphthoid ulcers early, and grading severity consistently, is exactly what treat-to-target care needs. CrohnSight automates the first pass and keeps the clinician in control: every prediction is shown with its uncertainty and a heat-map.

Interim build. The original plan (onset 5–6 years before diagnosis and disease-course prediction from hospital records) is on hold, not dropped: it needs MIMIC-IV, whose credentialed access is still pending. This build delivers the severity side of the project on five open datasets.
